A writer that starts late is counted, a capture's growth is a verdict, the verifier sees what ran

A real Rust run confirmed its documented leak in 5 of 167 minutes: a list that takes values only when
a bounded ring is full, with no consumer started. Three general defects were in the way:

Coverage of one step missed a writer that starts after a threshold:

- The Rust and Go coverage process ran one round of `step` units. The ring was not full yet, so the
  writer lines counted 0 and `dla trend` read `writer_not_executed` in every series of the driver made
  for the finding while its heap grew.
- When every writer line of a target counted 0, the coverage process now runs again for the length of
  the measured process (the units the driver did are read back from its checkpoints), and the run
  record says so. On the real driver: 3520 units, the writer lines counted 2877, 959 and 960 times
  (0, 0 and 0 in the run), and the target read `executed`.

A capture series gave its harness's targets no verdict:

- The allocation-stack series `dla summarize` writes from a harness's captures had `targets: []`, so
  a passed column tied to the finding never became `grew`, no trigger item was raised and the run
  ended complete with the public-API trigger never checked.
- Its tied targets are now `grew` (untied ones get no verdict), the trigger rule reads it as growth
  of its harness, and `dla run status` holds the run until an entry-path harness checks the trigger.

The verifier chose a non-leak reading against the measurements:

- `agents/verifier.md` has rules for readings that say the level stops or the memory is given back:
  a growing alloc-test, a doubling block, a remover that runs only on a condition, a stated purpose.
- `dla ledger verify` refuses `N-WARM` and `N-HWM` (`verify-contradicts-measurement`) when an
  alloc-test on the finding measured a live heap column still growing, unless the mechanism cites it.
- `dla bundle` gives the verifier, for each harness behind the evidence (at most four), its driver
  without the target block and `ran.txt`, the project functions that ran in its measured program
  (with the coverage length, a note when the list is cut or the length is unknown, and the loaded
  classes for a JVM harness). A reading that needs a drain must find it there.
- On the real run's bundle, three reruns each: before `N-HWM`, `N-HWM`, `N-INTENT`, all assuming a
  consumer that drains; now `L-COLLECT`, `L-COLLECT`, `N-RELEASE`, all naming the consumer that never
  started.
