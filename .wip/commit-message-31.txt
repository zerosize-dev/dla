Unexplained heap growth leads the run, and a Node heap summary shows who keeps the objects

From the miss on a Node library written in TypeScript. The first harness
measured the documented leak: the heap rose by about 15 KB per unit and
eight listener columns grew at lines of the sources. The gate had the
right item, a capture of that harness, but the residual row that raises it
is written only by `dla gap --residual`, a command the model never ran. It
worked through 79 queue harnesses and stopped with nothing confirmed and
no capture taken. Replayed by hand, the capture took 44 s, and its heap
summary named the tool's own agent as the top holder: every growing
object's path went through the list of objects a `WeakRef` was read from
in the current job, which the agent's own listener count fills just
before the snapshot.

- `dla trend <S>` writes the residual rows of its series, as `dla gap <S>
  --residual` does. When a heap row is over its threshold, its growth is
  over the floor and its harness has no summarized capture, the first
  `next` entry is the capture command and `_attention` says the heap
  grows unexplained.
- While such an item is open, `dla harness gen --next` is refused with
  `capture-first`, whose fix is the capture and the summaries it prints. A
  harness generated for a named finding (`--probe`, `--entry`) is not
  refused. The item comes before the queue item in both gates, in `dla
  run status` and in the stop hook. It is kept per harness. The
  benchmark's fixed policy takes the capture for this refusal as it does
  for the other two capture rules.
- The Node heap snapshot reader skips the edge `weak_refs_keep_during_job`
  of the strong root list as it skips weak edges, and counts in `notes`
  the objects only that edge reached. The statics of the agent's scripts
  and the agent's own symbol fields are no holders. On the replayed
  capture the first holders are now the library's socket objects, each
  with a path from the runtime's HTTP parser, and none of 200 holders
  has the job edge in its path. Moving the snapshot before the agent's
  reading did not remove the edge, so the preload is unchanged.
- `dla ledger close-rest` leaves a candidate that one flat run settled and
  that waits for its second look, with the reason, while the first pass of
  its queue is still going and the second look has room. In a real Rust
  run it had closed seven such candidates before their second look.
- The references say what each per-site count column counts: for
  `node.listeners@` the listeners registered at that line that are still
  attached to objects still alive after a forced collection; the model of
  the replayed run had read the column as a count of calls.

Existing tests whose scenes grew by accident over the floor now grow less
(one unit in eight or sixteen reaches the holder); tests whose growth is
the point take the capture first. No assertion was weakened. The whole
dynamic acceptance passes: 63 of 64 fixtures and the expected failure.

Not covered. The capture is per harness: a run whose harnesses all show
unexplained heap growth takes one capture per harness before each next
batch, and the benchmark does not measure that cost, as its policy never
asks for the next batch itself. The output of `dla trend` is close to its
size bound in many scenes. A short series with a real leak now leads
with the capture rather than with `SERIES TOO SHORT`. The refusal was
tested on a Python scene only.
