Measure the ranked candidates before closing any; Go heap captures on the bytes-allocated axis

Found by running the plugin on real libraries at versions with a documented leak. The agent
closed every static candidate as not exercised within minutes, with most of the time budget
left: the tool printed that close as the fix for each open candidate, and every "measure
before you close" rule was per runtime, never per candidate.

Measurement queue
- Candidates are ranked (a DLA rule or known-leak pattern; a census holder with writers and
  no removers in a registration table, container field or static; another detector hit; a
  census holder with writers; test, example, benchmark and documentation code last), and
  `dla ledger list` prints them in that order.
- `dla harness gen --driver --next [N]` declares the next pending candidates, 8 at most, as
  targets of the harness and writes above the driver's hooks what each one is: site, holder,
  owner, the rule's message, writers and removers with their functions, what its probe
  reads and what a unit of work must do to reach it. `--probe F` declares a target too, and
  a finding no probe can read is still a target.
- `dla trend` gives every target a verdict: grew, changed, constant, not_loaded, executed,
  not_executed, or unknown when the run has no coverage data.
- A ranked candidate is settled when a verdict is grew or changed, when a driver that
  targeted it saw its code run, or after two different drivers targeted it. While the queue
  of a runtime is open (time left above the floor, the workload not blocked, candidates
  pending), closing a pending candidate with not_exercised, no_harness or time_budget is
  refused (`target-pending`) with the batch command as the fix. The queue leads
  `dla run status`, the final gate and the stop hook; only a blocker recorded on
  `intake.workload` closes it.
- `dla ledger close-rest` closes what the queue does not hold back, through the rules of
  `dla ledger resolve`; a candidate whose column grew stays open for `dla ledger test`.
- The report has a `Queue:` line and says for each static-only or unresolved finding how
  it was targeted.
- `dla run init --queue off` keeps the earlier behaviour; the fixed policy of
  `dla-bench dynamic` uses it.
- The skill's checklist, rules, stage and recovery references and the worker agent follow
  the queue loop.
With this build the plugin, run on a Python HTTP library, confirmed the documented leak
(an unbounded cache on a module-level function) about five minutes into the run and went
on with the next batches; the build before closed all 187 candidates in five minutes.

Go heap profiles
- In command mode the heap profile of a point is the one of its last in-run sample, taken
  at a varying share of the run, while the capture series used the capture number as x. A
  leak that is linear in the work could read as slowing down, and the growth rule (the last
  step at least half of the first) failed by chance. On ten kept capture sets of two
  fixtures the second-half over first-half ratio of the leak's stack read 0.81 to 1.82.
- x is now the number of bytes the process had allocated when the profile was taken (the
  sum of alloc_space in the profile); the same ratio reads 0.90 to 1.19. The rule compares
  rates over x, the alloc-stack evidence reads the same positions, and a slope of such a
  series is bytes kept per byte allocated.
- Captures are put in the order of that total, since each profile of a separate process is
  one reading. Without positive, distinct totals x stays the capture number and a
  limitation line says why.
