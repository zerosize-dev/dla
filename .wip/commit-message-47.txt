One block whose bytes grow is growth in every summary of dumps and allocation stacks

The Rust library run's leak keeps one `Vec<u64>` that grows by `push`. Its allocation stacks
showed one live block at the push line whose bytes doubled, and `dla summarize alloc-stack-diff`
failed it: the summaries' rule held both the bytes and the block count to rise at every pair of
captures, and one block that is resized keeps a count of one. The same rule reads every runtime's
dumps and stacks, so a leak into one growing block never passed any of them: a Rust `Vec`, a Go
slice or `bytes.Buffer`, a Java `long[]`, `byte[]`, `ByteArrayOutputStream` or `StringBuilder`, a
Python `bytearray`.

- The dump-series rule passes a count that is the same positive number at every dump when the
  bytes rise at every pair and the last interval passes its share of the first. The passed column
  says `"resized": true`. A count that rises at some pairs and holds at others, a count of zero, and
  a flat pair of bytes still fail.
- The holder discriminator reads such a series as a leak; one that rose and ends flat reads
  inconclusive (a resized block is flat between resizes, and the alloc-test over a longer window
  decides), not non-leak. A series flat from its first dump, and a count that rose and then
  stopped, stay non-leak.
- A failed summary whose holder kept one block that grew but stood still between two dumps says
  so, and asks for captures whose work differs by a factor of two or more.
- A Rust harness takes its allocation stacks at 1, 2, 4 and 8 times its step (15 rounds against
  10), so a capacity that doubles is reallocated between every pair of points; the summary judges
  the stacks against the captures' own units of work.
- A passing, tied growth series that `dla summarize` wrote from the capture files of a driver made
  for the finding is a lead of that finding, as a heap-dump holder already is: the final gate
  lists it with the alloc-test, `ledger resolve` refuses to close it as "ran and showed no
  growth", `close-rest` leaves it open, and an alloc-test that favors non-leak lifts it. A capture
  of a test loop, of the project's own workload or of a driver made for other findings is no lead.

The trend gate's step reading of the last commit also changes:

- A series whose last reading falls below its highest by more than the noise tolerance is a fall,
  not a flat end, and is not rescued as a stair.
- Two steps are separate when a point that is not a rise lies between them, counted in points, so
  a ramp with one skipped sample is still a ramp.
- Once a capture was taken and not summarized, the trend leads with the summary that clears the
  item (allocation stacks or the heap graph), never another summary command.

The in-run capture points of the JVM, Node, Go and Python agents keep their even spacing; with
their default four dumps, a container that doubles can still be equal at the last pair, and the
summary then says what to do.
